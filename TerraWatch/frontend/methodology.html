How TerraWatch works

From satellite radar inputs to map-based screening and location reports

04 / METHODOLOGY
1

Radar observations

The backend ingests paired ascending and descending NISAR GUNW observations when available. It uses unwrapped interferometric phase and coherence/connected-component masks to form displacement estimates. Scene dates and the stored-pass count matter: the current interface may have only a small number of observations.

2

Displacement grid

The map displays backend raster outputs in centimetres on a nominal 80 m grid. A selected point returns its cell value, a local neighborhood mean, an uncertainty estimate, and a coherence-related status. Negative values are treated as sinking by the backend risk rules.

3

Risk screening

The backend classifies point displacement using its configured thresholds: high subsidence at or below −0.50 cm, moderate sinking at or below −0.25 cm, and uplift/swelling at or above +0.30 cm. These are prototype screening categories—not building safety ratings.

4

Spatial model outputs

An Isolation Forest produces a relative anomaly score from location and displacement values. DBSCAN groups adjacent pixels at or below −0.45 cm into spatial clusters. An anomaly score or cluster is a model output, not a probability of failure or a confirmed damage observation.

5

Structures and nearby context

Mapped structure features are sampled against the displacement raster and ranked by the backend. Point analysis also returns mapped exposed structures within 150 m when available. The list depends on the coverage and quality of those source features.

6

Time series and forecast

Historical charts show only dates and values returned by the backend; missing observations are not interpolated. The forecast extrapolates from the neighborhood mean and uncertainty. With fewer than five passes, the backend labels long-term confidence as low.

What the measurements mean

Interferometric SAR (InSAR) compares the phase of radar returns from repeat satellite observations to estimate relative displacement along the radar line of sight. TerraWatch presents the backend’s vertical-displacement product and point-analysis interpretation; results remain sensitive to viewing geometry, surface coherence, atmospheric effects, reference choices, and processing quality. A single cell is not a direct measurement of a particular building’s foundation movement.

Use the results responsibly

  • Use the map and report to prioritize follow-up—not to diagnose a structure remotely.
  • Check the reported confidence, uncertainty, observation count, and acquisition dates.
  • Annualized rates derived from short intervals are not observed long-term rates.
  • Forecast bands are model estimates, not guarantees or safety thresholds.
  • Confirm concerning results with qualified field inspection and appropriate geotechnical data.

TerraWatch is a research and demonstration interface. Data availability and model limitations apply; outputs are not a substitute for professional engineering assessment or emergency guidance.
